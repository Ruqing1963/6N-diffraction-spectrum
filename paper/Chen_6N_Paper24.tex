\begin{abstract}
Parts XX--XXII derived, in closed form, the spatial correlation of twin centres on
the $6N$ skeleton as an infinite product $R(j)=\prod_{q>3}R_q(j)$ of quantized
single-frequency interference factors. This paper renders that closed form
directly: evaluating the three interference states across the spatial-frequency
spectrum produces a two-panel \emph{diffraction spectrum} of the lattice---a
heat map of the single-frequency tensor $R_q(j)$ and the cumulative correlation
$R(j)$. The picture makes three established analytic facts legible at a glance:
the $O(1/q^2)$ decay of high-frequency amplitude (so low primes dominate the
structure); the maximal two-body \emph{suppression} at $j=1$, where the cumulative
product falls to $R(1)=\mathfrak{S}_{\rm quad}/\mathfrak{S}_{\rm twin}^2\approx0.41$
(strictly positive---a suppressed but admissible quadruplet, \emph{not}
annihilation); and the multi-frequency \emph{super-resonance} at $j=35=5\cdot7$,
where State~A fires at both $q=5$ and $q=7$ and $R(35)\approx2.26$. The rendering
is an illustration of the analytic $R(j)$, equal at integer lags to the discrete
singular-series ratio; it is not new prime data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
In Parts XX through XXII, we established that the spatial correlation between twin
centres at distance $j$ is not random but is governed by a circular convolution of
castellated indicators on the phase-space tori, with the global correlation given
by the absolutely convergent product $R(j)=\prod_{q>3}R_q(j)$ and a finite spectral
cutoff $k^\star(\varepsilon)$~\cite{ChenXX,ChenXXI,ChenXXII}.

In this paper we render that closed-form correlation visually. Plotting the tensor
of single-frequency interference states and their cumulative product yields the
``diffraction spectrum'' of the lattice---a compact way to read off the
small-prime dominance, the suppression and resonance lags, and the quasi-periodic
structure that the analytic results predict.

\section{The Tensor Construction}

We construct the single-frequency interference tensor $\mathbf{R}=[R_q(j)]$ for
twin--twin correlation. Following the exact convolution states of
Part~XX~\cite{ChenXX}, each entry takes one of three values according to the
shift $j$ and frequency $q$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{State A (Constructive Resonance):} $R_q(j) = \frac{q}{q-2}$ when $j \equiv 0 \pmod q$.
    \item \textbf{State B (Partial Interference):} $R_q(j) = \frac{q(q-3)}{(q-2)^2}$ when $j \equiv \pm 3^{-1} \pmod q$.
    \item \textbf{State C (Destructive Misalignment):} $R_q(j) = \frac{q(q-4)}{(q-2)^2}$ for all other $j$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Spectral decay}
Reading the heat map (Figure~\ref{fig:hologram}A) down the frequency axis
(increasing $q$), the colour fades rapidly toward neutrality ($R_q\approx1$). This
illustrates the absolute-convergence result of Part~XXII~\cite{ChenXXII}: away from
its finitely many resonances each frequency sits in State~C with deviation
$4/(q-2)^2=O(1/q^2)$, so the macroscopic structure of $R(j)$ is dominated by the
lowest frequencies ($q\le13$) while the high-frequency tail contributes a small,
summable correction.

\subsection{Maximal suppression at $j=1$}
At $j=1$---two twin centres one step apart, i.e.\ a prime quadruplet---no prime is
in a constructive state: State~A needs $q\mid1$ and State~B needs $q\mid(3\mp1)$,
both impossible for $q>3$, so \emph{every} frequency sits in State~C. The
cumulative product therefore reaches its minimum, $R(1)\approx0.41$, below even the
single-frequency floor $R_5(1)=5/9\approx0.556$.

This is the point most easily misread, so we state it carefully. $R(1)\approx0.41$
is a \emph{suppression}, not an annihilation: it is strictly positive, and equals
exactly the quadruplet prefactor
$R(1)=\mathfrak{S}_{\rm quad}/\mathfrak{S}_{\rm twin}^2$, the intercept-suppression
identified in Part~XX~\cite{ChenXX}. The quadruplet is an admissible constellation
with positive density; the dip merely says two adjacent twins are
\emph{anti-correlated} relative to independence. Genuine zero-measure annihilation
($R=0$) requires the dead-residue sets to \emph{tile} $\mathbb{Z}/q$, which a
twin pair can never achieve---the twin is immune to two-body annihilation, and the
lowest order at which annihilation occurs is three-body (Part~XXI)~\cite{ChenXXI}.
The $j=1$ trough is thus the deepest \emph{constructive-free} suppression, not a
collapse to zero.

\subsection{Multi-frequency super-resonance at $j=35$}
At $j=35=5\cdot7$, State~A (constructive, $R_q=q/(q-2)$) fires simultaneously at
$q=5$ and $q=7$, since both divide $35$. Their factors $\tfrac53$ and $\tfrac75$
multiply against an otherwise near-neutral tail, lifting the cumulative product to
its maximum in the window, $R(35)\approx2.26$. This is the constructive mirror of
the $j=1$ trough: a lag whose small-prime structure aligns the dead-residue sets
of the two centres rather than scattering them. Such resonances recur whenever $j$
is divisible by several small primes (the quasi-periodicity of $R(j)$).

\section{Conclusion}
Rendering the single-frequency tensor $R_q(j)$ and its cumulative product $R(j)$
makes the closed-form results of Parts~XX--XXII legible in one picture: a
quasi-periodic, small-prime-dominated correlation that behaves like a
multi-frequency diffraction pattern, with anti-correlated suppression troughs (the
deepest at $j=1$, the quadruplet prefactor $\mathfrak{S}_{\rm quad}/
\mathfrak{S}_{\rm twin}^2\approx0.41>0$) and constructive resonances (the strongest
here at $j=35=5\cdot7$, $R\approx2.26$). The rendering is an illustration of the
analytic correlation, equal at integer lags to the discrete singular-series ratio;
it adds no new prime data and makes no claim about the infinitude of any
constellation.
